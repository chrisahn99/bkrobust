# Substrate decisions for Table 2 (A1)

Written 2026-09-12 on branch `iclr2027-eval-plan`. Four items, each closed with
a decision, the files it touched, and what it leaves undone. Everything here is
additive: `results/frame`, `results/elicit` and `results/ledger` were read and
not rewritten, and `frame_build.py`, which has no dry-run flag and writes
`results/frame` unconditionally, was not run. The per-network table these
decisions feed is `TABLE2_SUBSTRATE.md`, generated by
`experiments/substrate_table.py`.

## 1. Sortability probes on the Gaussian tier (T3)

**What ran.** `experiments/sortability_probe.py`, n = 5000, seeds 0 to 9,
ancestral samples from the fitted parameters in the four bnlearn JSON files
(`arth150`, `ecoli70`, `magic-irri`, `magic-niab`): per node an intercept, one
coefficient per parent and a residual variance. The loader checks that every
node has a parameter block, that its coefficient keys are exactly its parents in
the arc list, and that every residual variance is positive; all four files pass.
Var-sortability follows Reisach, Seiler and Weichwald (NeurIPS 2021,
arXiv:2102.13647, section 3.1); R2-sortability follows Reisach, Tami, Seiler,
Chambaz and Weichwald (NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2303.18211, equation 2) with the
least-squares R2 of each variable on all the others. Both are implemented in
the probe from the printed definitions (`order_alignment`, about thirty lines,
and `r2_on_all_others`); CausalDisco is not imported. The functional counts one
term per ordered pair and path *length*, as both papers specify, so a pair
joined by two paths of equal length is one term; the term count is written to
`sortability.json` as `n_path_terms`. Ties count one half; none occurred.
`tests/benchmarks/test_sortability_probe.py` pins the functional to the 2021
paper's worked example (3/4), to the tie rule, to the one-term-per-length rule,
and to the population value on a 200,000-row sample.

**Results** (`sortability.json`; mean over seeds, sd, range, and the population
value computed from the exact covariance `(I - B)^-1 Omega (I - B)^-T`):

| network | nodes | edges | terms | var-sortability | population | R2-sortability | population |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|---|---:|
| arth150 | 107 | 150 | 794 | 0.624 +- 0.004 (0.617 to 0.627) | 0.627 | 0.252 +- 0.006 (0.242 to 0.262) | 0.251 |
| ecoli70 | 46 | 70 | 333 | 0.708 +- 0.007 (0.700 to 0.718) | 0.718 | 0.379 +- 0.013 (0.360 to 0.393) | 0.390 |
| magic-irri | 64 | 102 | 515 | 0.592 +- 0.005 (0.581 to 0.598) | 0.598 | 0.614 +- 0.015 (0.596 to 0.637) | 0.608 |
| magic-niab | 44 | 66 | 184 | 0.327 +- 0.005 (0.321 to 0.337) | 0.326 | 0.764 +- 0.007 (0.750 to 0.772) | 0.766 |

**Reading.** None of the four sits near one on either criterion, which is
where both papers place the simulated benchmarks they were written about.
Var-sortability runs from 0.33 to 0.71; on `magic-niab` it is below one half,
so variance falls along the causal order more often than it rises. R2-sortability
runs from 0.25 to 0.76 and does not track var-sortability across the four
(`arth150` has the second-highest var- and the lowest R2-sortability). Seed
dispersion is at most 0.015 and every mean is within about one sd of its
population value. The sampler check, the largest relative deviation of any
sample variance from its population value, is 0.07 on every network; the sd of a
single sample variance at n = 5000 is about 0.02, and 0.07 is the size expected
for the maximum over roughly a thousand draws, so it is sampling noise, not a
bug. Consequence for the plan: the sampling band on the Gaussian tier can be
run without a sortability control, and an estimated CPDAG on these four
networks cannot be read off the variances alone.

**UNDEFINED.** The other 37 files: 24 BIF networks (discrete conditional
probability tables, no continuous parameters), 12 dagitty diagrams (structure
only; `M-bias.txt` in addition is not a DAG), and the new `M-structure.txt`.
Each is written to `sortability.json` with its reason. `sachs` in particular is
UNDEFINED on the object in the tree, the discretised BIF; the continuous
flow-cytometry data would define it but are not in the repository
(`docs/DATA.md`, "Obtained" unticked).

**Undone.** No continuous parameters were fitted to the discrete networks; that
would be a modelling choice, not a probe. `src/bkrobust/data/leakage.py` is
still a stub (`NotImplementedError`) and was left alone; the probe is the only
implementation in the tree, and moving it under `src/` is a separate change.

## 2. The M-bias fixture

**What the file is.** `M-bias.txt` (pgmpy 1.0.0 sdist, sha256
`54e23d91...6297f6`) is dagitty's M-bias example: three nodes E, Z, D; one
arc E -> D; two bidirected edges E <-> Z and Z <-> D, dagitty's notation for
an unmeasured common cause. The parser records them in `bidirected`, and
`describe_network` and `frame_build` refuse to treat the file as a DAG (status
`not_a_dag`, zero frame pairs, zero ledger rows). Under the frozen protocol the
M-bias illustration contributes nothing and the table said so only implicitly.

**The expansion.** The canonical DAG of the ADMG replaces each bidirected edge
by a fresh parent of both endpoints: U1 -> E, U1 -> Z for E <-> Z; U2 -> Z,
U2 -> D for Z <-> D; E -> D kept. Five nodes, five arcs: the M-structure
(Greenland, Epidemiology 2003). Its CPDAG has two v-structures, U1 -> Z <- U2
and E -> D <- U2, which compel four arcs; E - U1 is the one undirected edge and
the one chain component (size 2). The frame rule of `frame_build.py`
(treatments in or beside a component of size at least two, outcomes among
possible descendants), applied through its own `treatments()` and
`possible_descendants` without writing `results/frame`, yields six pairs:
(E, D), (E, U1), (E, Z), (U1, D), (U1, E), (U1, Z).

**Causal-sufficiency argument.** A DAG over a variable set V is causally
sufficient when every common cause of two variables in V is itself in V. In
the expansion the only common causes of any pair are U1 (of E and Z) and U2 (of
Z and D), both in V' = {U1, U2, E, Z, D}, and the ADMG asserts no further
latent structure; so the expansion is causally sufficient with respect to V'
by construction, and its latent projection back onto {E, Z, D}, marginalising
U1 and U2, is exactly the ADMG in `M-bias.txt`. Nothing structural was added or
lost. What changes is the meaning. The content of the M-bias example is that U1
and U2 are *unmeasured*: an analyst who adjusts for Z opens
E <- U1 -> Z <- U2 -> D and biases an effect the empty set identifies. In this
protocol every node of a DAG is observable: the CPDAG is over all nodes, the
questionnaire may ask about E - U1, and a committed set may contain U1 or U2. On
the expansion the optimal set for (E, D) is {U2}, parents of the causal node D
minus the forbidden set, valid whichever way E - U1 is oriented, so no
background knowledge can make it invalid and the M-bias trap does not exist.
That is not a defect of the expansion; it is what "measured" means. The fixture
therefore illustrates two things in the protocol's own terms: a calibration
point on which every certificate should read `safe` or `slack` on every arm,
like the empty-knowledge arm; and, by contrast with the ADMG it came from, the
boundary of the framework: a query whose validity depends on a latent needs a
PAG-level criterion and radius, which the repository does not have.

**Decision: INCLUDE the expansion as a separate T0 fixture, named for what it
is; keep the ADMG untouched and excluded.** Added
`results/axisa3/networks/example_models/M-structure.txt` with the provenance in
its header (source file and hash, the expansion rule, the positions taken from
dagitty's edge control points). `M-bias.txt` is unchanged and still refused by
the parsers, so the table prints the exclusion rather than converting the file
silently. Registered in `experiments/ledger_sweep.py` (`TIER`, T0) and
`experiments/questionnaire_build.py` (`DOMAIN`, the same sentence as the other
T0 diagrams). Parsers: `parse_dagitty_file` accepts it (comment lines are
dropped before the header check), `describe_network` returns `ok`, one
undirected edge, component sizes (2,). It is not in the pgmpy acquisition
manifest, which lists the 40 sdist files and their hashes, so
`describe.parse_corpus`, which iterates the manifest, will not see it;
`frame_build`, `questionnaire_build`, `knowledge_audit` and `elicit_run`, which
iterate the directory, will pick it up on their next run. Until then it is on
disk and outside the frozen frame, the questionnaire and the ledger; entering
it is a frame rebuild, a dated amendment to the freeze, and is not made here.

The alternative, a written exclusion only, was not taken: the exclusion already
holds mechanically, and the argument above is what shows the expansion to be a
different object, which the table needs a row to say.

## 3. The selection node in Didelez_2010

**What the file says.** Seven nodes. `S [selected]` with arcs Age -> S and
TCI -> S; HRT `exposure`, TCI `outcome`, Age and Smo `adjusted`. Didelez,
Kreiner and Keiding (Statistical Science 2010) is about inference under
outcome-dependent sampling, and in dagitty's grammar `selected` marks a variable
the data are conditioned on: S is the inclusion indicator of a sample selected
on the outcome and on age. The parser keeps the role (`roles["S"] ==
("selected",)`, since `_ROLES` lists it) and nothing downstream reads it:
`ParsedNetwork.roles` reaches neither `frame.jsonl` nor `rows.csv`. The
questionnaire's domain sentence for this network does say "selection into the
study", so the suppliers were told what S is.

**What S does structurally.** CPDAG of the seven-node DAG: eight directed arcs,
three undirected edges (Age - Occ, Occ - Smo, Occ - Thist), one chain component
{Age, Occ, Smo, Thist}. S is a sink outside the component with both of its arcs
compelled. Removing S and recomputing the CPDAG over the remaining six nodes
gives exactly the restriction of the seven-node CPDAG: the same six directed
arcs, the same three undirected edges, the same component. S changes no
orientation anywhere; it only adds itself as a possible outcome.

**What it does to the rows.** Frame: 24 pairs with S in the graph, of which four
have S as outcome (Age -> S, Occ -> S, Smo -> S, Thist -> S); the cap of 20 by
seeded stride (step 1, the first twenty in sorted order) kept the first three and
dropped the four Thist pairs (Thist -> Occ, Thist -> S, Thist -> Smo,
Thist -> TCI). Ledger, `results/ledger/rows.csv`: **320 rows on Didelez_2010, 20
pairs by 16 arms; 48 rows have S as outcome** (three pairs by 16 arms); no row
has S as treatment; S can enter no committed set for another outcome, because
it is a sink and the optimal set is built from parents of causal nodes. Without
S the frame is exactly 20 pairs, the cap would not bind, and the three Thist
pairs now dropped would enter: the block would stay at 320 rows with 48 swapped
for 48 others.

**Decision: RECOVERED AS ORDINARY, with the 48 rows named as a stratum.** (a) S
is a node of the DAG its authors drew; in the source paper's own graphical
treatment the selection indicator is a vertex one conditions on, and as a
structural object the seven-node graph is a DAG whose CPDAG on the other six
nodes does not depend on it. (b) The audit is at the level of the DAG and has no
selection semantics anywhere; it never conditions on S = 1, so the 272 rows
with another outcome are scored exactly as they would be without S. (c) The 48
rows in which S is the outcome ask for the effect of Age, Occ or Smo on
selection into the study, a query no analyst of this study poses: legitimate
DAG queries, wrong applied ones. They stay in the frozen ledger and are declared
here as the stratum `selection_indicator_outcome` (network Didelez_2010, y = S,
48 rows), so any per-tier or per-network summary can be printed with and without
them. A `roles` column on the ledger rows would make the flag mechanical; it is
not added here because it changes `ledger_sweep.py`'s row schema.

## 4. The Sachs check

**Edge for edge.** `sachs.bif.gz` in the tree (sha256 `ed1830fb...01bce`) is
byte-identical to the file bnlearn serves today
(`bnlearn.com/bnrepository/sachs/sachs.bif.gz`, fetched 2026-09-12). Its 17
arcs are exactly the model string bnlearn publishes for the network validated
in Sachs et al. (Science 2005):
`[PKC][PKA|PKC][Raf|PKC:PKA][Mek|PKC:PKA:Raf][Erk|Mek:PKA][Akt|Erk:PKA]`
`[P38|PKC:PKA][Jnk|PKC:PKA][Plcg][PIP3|Plcg][PIP2|Plcg:PIP3]`. Seventeen of
seventeen match, none reversed, none missing:

PKC -> PKA, PKC -> Raf, PKC -> Mek, PKC -> Jnk, PKC -> P38, PKA -> Raf,
PKA -> Mek, PKA -> Erk, PKA -> Akt, PKA -> Jnk, PKA -> P38, Raf -> Mek,
Mek -> Erk, Erk -> Akt, Plcg -> PIP3, Plcg -> PIP2, PIP3 -> PIP2.

**But "the consensus network" is two objects in the literature.** The Tetrad
group's ground truth (`cmu-phil/example-causal-datasets`,
`real/sachs/ground.truth/sachs.2005.ground.truth.graph.txt`, fetched
2026-09-12) has the same 11 nodes and 20 edges: it shares 16 with the BIF,
reverses one (PIP3 -> PLCg where the BIF has Plcg -> PIP3) and adds three
(PIP2 -> PKC, PIP3 -> Akt, PLCg -> PKC). That 20-edge graph is the signalling
map the paper starts from; the 17-arc graph is the network the paper's
Bayesian-network analysis inferred from the data, and the difference between
the two, three arcs and one reversal, is the paper's own account of what the
inference missed. bnlearn distributes the inferred one, with conditional
probability tables fitted to the discretised data.

**Decision: ONE object, provided the paper names it.** The anchor is the 17-arc
network inferred in Sachs et al. (2005) as distributed by bnlearn, with the
tables fitted to discretised data; the word "consensus" is not to be used
unqualified, and "true DAG" not at all (`docs/DATA.md` already says why). Two
facts make the naming load-bearing rather than pedantic. First, the CPDAG of
this graph is fully undirected (17 of 17, components of 8 and 3): no v-structure
survives, so every one of its 17 orientations is a knowledge claim. Second, the
single edge on which the two published versions disagree, Plcg - PIP3, lies in
the size-3 component {Plcg, PIP2, PIP3}, so a supplier reciting the signalling
map is scored as making a false claim against this truth. That happened. In
`results/elicit/answers_*.json` (item 139, orientations as stated, before the
Meek repair), five arms assert PIP3 -> Plcg, the map's direction: the 72B model
on both presentation orders (confidence 0.6 and 0.8), gemma-27B (0.8), and the
scrambled-name arms at 7B (1.0) and 72B (0.5); two assert Plcg -> PIP3, the
BIF's direction: the 7B model on its second presentation order (1.0) and the
70B second-family model (0.5); three decline the pair: the primary 7B arm, the
32B model and the 8B second family. Whichever object is called the truth, one
group of arms is wrong on that edge; the commission counts on `sachs` are
therefore a property of the choice made here, and the paper must state the
choice. On tiering: `ledger_sweep.TIER` lists `sachs` as T1 (a BIF); if
Table 2 presents it as the real-data anchor of T3, the tier map and the table
have to be reconciled, which is not done here.

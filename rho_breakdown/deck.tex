\begin{frame}[shrink=12]{2. Locality is the finding, not a speed-up}
\S8.2 lists ``prune by relevance'' among the computational mitigations. That quotient is the result.

\smallskip
Censoring of $\rho^\ast_{\text{any}}$ at $n = 20\,000$, by minimum hop distance from an elicited
statement to $\{X, Y\}$ on the CPDAG skeleton:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
ensemble & dist 0 & dist 1 & dist $\ge 2$ \\
\midrule
\texttt{original} ($p$ 5--8)   & \textbf{0.037} \tiny(2\,539) & \textbf{1.000} \tiny(93)  & \\
\texttt{licensed} ($p$ 5--10)  & \textbf{0.070} \tiny(1\,852) & \textbf{0.935} \tiny(229) & \\

\bottomrule

\end{center}

\medskip
\ok{13 to 27 fold.} Invariant in $n$ to four decimals (0.9345 at $n = 2\,000$, $20\,000$, $\infty$).
Exactly 1.000 for all 331 \texttt{large} SCMs two or more hops out.

\medskip
$\rho^\ast$ is a functional of $O^\ast$ and $O^\ast$ is local, so a statement that does not reach the
query cannot move it. No sample size repairs that.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{2b. What locality buys, and one anomaly it dissolves}
\begin{block}{The second currency the abstract is missing}
Elicited knowledge far from the query does not move the estimate. \textbf{Stop paying to elicit
it.} A cost that goes down, with a number, is one of the two claims that survive adjustment for
domain.
\end{block}

\medskip
It also dissolves something we were both treating as a separate regime. \texttt{large} behaves
differently because only \textbf{60.3\,\%} of its SCMs have a statement at distance 0, against
88.7\,\% (\texttt{licensed}) and 96.3\,\% (\texttt{original}).

\medskip
Condition on distance 0 and it stops being a different regime.

\medskip
\wn{Your 22 August note reaches the same place from the other side}: if influence is local, one false
statement touches $O(1)$ of $O(d^2)$ queries, so mean damage tends to zero as the graph grows.
Aggregate worst-case or distributionally, never on the mean.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{7b. And the ignorance does not shrink with data}
$SE_n \propto n^{-1/2}$, while $d(\rho)$ is free of $n$. So the two do not converge, they diverge.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
$n$ & 200 & 1\,000 & 5\,000 & 20\,000 \\
\midrule
mean robust / naive width ratio & 1.61 & 2.32 & 3.93 & \textbf{6.84} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\medskip
This is the best answer we have to ``collect more data''. Structural uncertainty about the
orientations is not a small-sample problem that goes away, and at $n = 20\,000$ it dominates
the sampling noise by a factor of seven on the mean.

\medskip
\wn{With the caveat from the next slide: the \emph{median} ratio is 1.00, because the ball is inert
for 69\,\% of problems. Print both.}
\end{frame}

## What the halt rule found (pre-registration Appendix A.0b)

The first table showed the claim certificate dangerous on 7 rows. Inspection: **6 were a
bucket bug** (a search censored by the 300-subset budget was scored as an unbounded radius; now
scored at its floor `r_claim ≥ d`), **1 was an oracle bug in the repo**: `is_valid_adjustment_set_dag`
is Pearl's back-door criterion, which forbids every descendant of X; the certificate decides with
the generalised adjustment criterion. Independent networkx implementation sided with the
certificate; the back-door oracle rejects 4 of 1,169 valid committed sets. The audit now uses
`bkrobust.benchmarks.audit.is_valid_adjustment_set_gac_dag` (guard test
`tests/benchmarks/test_audit_oracle.py`). The 29 hop rows stay: **the unit gap measured**
(r_claim = 1, r_hop = 2, one false claim, set invalid: retracting the claim from the CPDAG
removes its propagations at once; the hop walk needs two steps).

Also fixed on the way: the E1 ladder's CP-SAT model is built over every ordered node triple and
never returns on 223 nodes (the first sweep hung on `andes`); the hop instrument now walks
exhaustively when ≤ 12 edges are retractable, to depth 3 otherwise, ladder only ≤ 60 nodes,
4,000-closure budget, censored rows carry `r_hop ≥ depth + 1`. `D_RAND` seeding used Python's
salted `hash()` and was not reproducible across processes; now sha256 of the network name.

## What the paper can now say, in licensed form

- The claim-unit certificate never over-certifies, by construction, and this was checked on
  1,524 deployment rows with an audit that shares no code with it. The hop-unit certificate
  over-certifies on 1.5–3.6 % of rows; the naive counts on 17–34 %.
- Paired by network, the claim certificate is as safe as the free hop-count rule and sharper by
  0.21 [0.05, 0.38] in false alarms on the primary supplier (wins on four of five deployment
  arms, underpowered on the second family); the hop radius is sharper still by 0.14 but unsafe.
- A 7B/8B supplier is wrong on a third of what it asserts and its committed set is invalid at
  the truth on a quarter to a third of certifiable queries; chance is at 0.38. On 24 networks
  the model beats chance on commission (paired) and is not shown to beat it on validity.
- Knowledge buys identifiability first: 68 → ~200–290 certifiable queries.
- The certificate counts claims between the set and its failure; it is a property of the
  asserted set's shape around the query, not of its truth (L7). Say so.

\section{Evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

We evaluate on three families of causal structures: benchmark Bayesian networks, directed acyclic
graphs published in applied papers together with the constraints their authors stated, and networks
whose parameters are fit to measured data. For each network we estimate a CPDAG from one designated
discovery run and draw treatment-outcome pairs from that estimate, fixing the candidate set before
any knowledge is collected so that every knowledge source is evaluated on the same queries and the
same denominator. Background knowledge is elicited through a questionnaire derived from the
estimated graph, which asks for a causal order over each chain component rather than for individual
edge directions, since pairwise queries yield cyclic answers at scale. Four sources answer it
independently: a language model queried at temperature zero with the response cached against a hash
of the rendered prompt; a replicate that varies either the presentation order or the model family,
so that instrument variance and source variance are separated; knowledge read from each network's
own source publication, with a verbatim quotation required for every assertion; and orientations
drawn uniformly among the choices Meek closure accepts, matched to the elicited sets in size,
granularity and distance from the treatment, which distinguishes a wide radius earned by correct
knowledge from one earned by volume and placement. The radius is computed from
$(\widehat{C}, K, \widehat{\Sigma}, n)$ alone, and is reported against the simpler statistics an
analyst already has, including the hop distance from the treatment to the nearest member of the
adjustment set. Networks rather than pairs are the unit of analysis. The generating graph is used to
draw samples and, once every reported quantity is fixed, to check whether the certificate held; it
never enters the computation.
